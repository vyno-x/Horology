The Evolution of the Mechanical Watch

For over two centuries, mechanical horology has bridged fine metallurgy, high-precision engineering, and artistic finishing. Unlike mass-produced electronic devices that degrade rapidly, a finely regulated mechanical movement—powered by an oscillating balance wheel or a Spring Drive tri-synchro regulator—is engineered to outlive generations.

Every watchmaker represented in our archive holds a distinct place in human history: Rolex pioneered the waterproof case for deep-sea and commercial diving; Omega timed mankind’s first steps on the lunar surface; Tudor democratized high-grade tool watches; and Grand Seiko brought Japanese artisanal perfection and micro-mechanics to the global forefront.
